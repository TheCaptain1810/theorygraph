# Product Brief

## Product
**Name:** TheoryGraph (working title)

**One-line description:** A collaborative, evidence-linked space where cinephiles explore how films create meaning through symbolism, cinematography, recurring motifs, and narrative structure.

## Problem hypothesis
Film analysis is spread across essays, social feeds, comment threads, videos, and personal notes. It can be difficult to connect a claim to the scene that inspired it, compare competing interpretations, or build on another fan's analysis.

## Target users
### Primary
Highly engaged cinephiles who:
- Write long-form reviews or film essays.
- Discuss symbolism, cinematography, editing, narrative ambiguity, and director intent.
- Participate in film communities.
- Want nuanced discussion rather than only ratings and short comments.

### Secondary (later)
- Film clubs and societies.
- Film students and educators.
- Critics and independent researchers.

## Job to be done
“When I have an interesting interpretation of a film, I want to connect it to specific moments, explore competing explanations, and discuss it with people who care about the details.”

## Core product loop
1. A user discovers a film or interpretation.
2. They read the claim and its supporting observations.
3. They add a counter-reading, additional evidence, or a thoughtful reply.
4. The discussion becomes more useful and discoverable.
5. Users share the page or return to explore connected ideas.

## Differentiation hypothesis
The product combines:
- Scene-linked claims and observations.
- Clear distinctions between observable details, inferences, fan theories, and externally supported factual claims.
- Competing interpretations that remain visible together.
- Spoiler-aware discovery.
- Future links between motifs, scenes, directors, cinematographers, and films.

No individual feature is assumed to be novel. Validate whether the combined workflow solves a real problem better than existing tools.

## Non-goals for MVP
- Movie ratings or generic review site.
- Hosting or streaming films.
- Uploading or redistributing copyrighted footage.
- Automated frame-by-frame video analysis.
- AI-generated theories at scale.
- Full social network, follower graph, or direct messaging.
- Native mobile apps.
- Graph database or elaborate graph visualization.
- Paid subscriptions.

## Success criteria for the first pilot
Suggested experimental goals, not industry benchmarks:
- 20 active pilot users.
- 30 substantive interpretations.
- At least 5 genuine counter-reading exchanges.
- Evidence that users return and contribute without repeated personal prompting.
- Qualitative feedback showing the structured workflow is useful compared with ordinary comments.

If users browse but do not contribute, the product may be a content site rather than a collaborative community. If users contribute but do not return, investigate the repeat-use value.
